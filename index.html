1. Your financial foundation

Use your real numbers. These example values are only a starting point.

Total cash and financial investments you want to include in this plan. Exclude borrowed money, home equity you cannot access, and money owed for taxes or bills. Do not count the same money twice.
Your average monthly income after taxes, including reliable job and business income. If income varies, use a conservative estimate.
Include housing, groceries, utilities, transportation, insurance and required debt payments. Leave out optional purchases.
Imagine your income stops today. How many months of essential bills should your cash cover? Three to six months is a common starting range; consider more for irregular income.
Only include balances you consider high interest, such as credit cards or costly personal loans. As a starting rule, consider debt around 8% APR or higher; compare its cost with safe after-tax returns. If all your debt is low interest, enter $0 here and include required payments in monthly spending.

2. Your savings and investment assumptions

Adjust these estimates to see how your strategy changes. You do not need to know the exact future rates.

Estimated annual interest on Treasury bills or savings, before taxes. Enter 4.5 for 4.5%. This is an assumption, not a live rate or a guaranteed return.
Estimated annual increase in living costs. For example, 3 means goods costing $100 today may cost about $103 next year.
Your estimated tax rate on interest income, not the tax rate on your entire account. Treasury interest is generally exempt from state and local income tax, but federally taxable. Use an estimate if unsure.
After setting aside protection money, choose the share available for risky assets. For example, 50% of $10,000 remaining means $5,000 for investing and $5,000 for savings. This is a budget, not a buy order.

3. Your protection check

Protect against losses that could force you to sell investments or take on expensive debt.

Consider health, auto, home or renters, disability, life and liability coverage where applicable. Check this only after reviewing your own needs; it does not mean every policy is required. This planning model uses the review as a readiness checkpoint.
